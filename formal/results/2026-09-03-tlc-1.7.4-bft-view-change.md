## Model and finite bounds

The model contains four equal-weight validators (`v1..v4`), exactly one
Byzantine validator (`v4`), a 3-of-4 QC/timeout threshold, four views (`0..3`),
two heights, an explicit leader function, two conflicting height-1 roots, one
height-2 child, persistent vote locks/highQC, crash/restart of one
representative honest validator, Byzantine leader equivocation, and stale
messages that normal QC/TC construction ignores.

The lagging-node subsystem accepts only received authenticated commit
certificates, exactly one next height at a time, whose parent root equals the
node's current root. It includes a valid `A1 -> A2` chain and an individually
certified but discontinuous `B1 -> A2` input set.

The safety state space is deliberately decomposed rather than multiplied:

- the full consensus graph exhausts all modeled single-height cross-view vote,
  lock, QC, TC, equivocation, stale-message, and representative crash/restart
  interleavings;
- the catch-up graph exhausts the two-height certificate/root combinations;
- the progress graph advances locks, highQC, commits, and catch-up across both
  heights under one explicit finite fair schedule.

The checked invariants include:

- no two commit QCs finalize different roots at the same height;
- no honest validator votes for two proposals in one view or two roots at one

- every honest vote was preceded by a durable vote-lock event;
- persistent lock height and persistent highQC height do not regress through
  crash/restart;
- every vote QC has three distinct modeled voters including at least two
  honest validators, so Byzantine plus stale messages cannot manufacture one;
- every timeout certificate has the same 3-of-4 / at-least-two-honest support;
- a committed child has a certified committed parent root;
- catch-up accepts only received certificates, has no height holes, and never
  crosses roots.

## Finite fair progress result

`ProgressSpec` encodes one 17-transition schedule:

```text
partitioned timeout -> network becomes synchronous -> Byzantine leader
equivocates -> valid timeout certificate -> honest leader/lock/QC/commit at h1
-> next valid timeout/view -> honest leader/lock/QC/commit at h2
-> receive both commit certificates -> catch up h1 -> catch up h2
```

TLC checked the temporal property `<>ProgressComplete` with weak fairness on
that schedule and completed its temporal SCC check. This proves only that the
explicit finite fair schedule reaches two commits and full catch-up within 17
modeled transitions. It does not prove general HotStuff/PBFT liveness,
eventual delivery bounds, leader rotation fairness, or progress under arbitrary
crash/partition schedules.

## Explicit limitations

The model does not implement HotStuff phases, real QC signatures, weighted or
dynamic validators, view-change message encoding, highQC selection among
competing certified branches, network buffers, replay windows, clocks,
adaptive corruption, multi-node simultaneous disk loss, HSM semantics, or
real catch-up storage/application. Equal weights and 3-of-4 quorums are fixed.
The normal consensus safety graph proves the cross-view single-height quorum
and lock boundary; the two-height path is checked separately by the catch-up
and finite progress configurations.

The current Rust symbols listed in `TRACEABILITY.md` are navigation anchors
only. This run does not establish that Rust implements these transitions or
invariants.
