# Ordered background recent-slot interface

An explicitly installed ordered Native-retained complete-frame hint may select
one still-unprepared direct current head in the existing recent-class position
of a background preparation. The first two FIFO offers, original historical
stream, total four-packet limit and original priority/background repayment
schedule remain intact. Every failed-carriage retry uses its original selection.

The preference applies only to an eligible exact frame already present in the
original recent stream, with a configured destination equal to this peer. It
cannot replace a historical place, a FIFO offer or a different class. Receipt,
visited-hop, route and authenticated-hop suppression checks still execute before
carriage. The complete original packet, routing and frame remain retained and
fully authenticated; a frame hint cannot authorize Native acceptance or signing.

One primitive peer-local debt bit uses the existing bounded process-local hint
pool under the complete Mesh carriage domain. A preferred current packet owes
one ordinary recent service on a later background preparation. Changing the
Native context, scope or head cannot clear that debt. Priority pairs and retries
do not repay it. Actual ordinary recent carriage repays it; a failed publication,
empty/tightened preparation or a history-only preparation does not. Missing or
evicted hints/debt use the ordinary path. Explicit hint installation can
initialize a missing debt, without rewriting any durable state.

Advance the debt only after original atomic preparation succeeds, and only from
actual carried recent packets. The preferred packet does not move the ordinary
recent frame/recipient ring past waiting background rows. All durable preparation
cursors still record actual carried bytes. Restart loses optional ordering only;
full cold verification and all original evidence, caller/signing heads, capacities,
quorum, maturity and deadlines remain mandatory.

This changes ground carriage scheduling only. Preparation is not connection,
remote or local reply custody, a destination receipt, ledger finality or recipient
spendability. Finite signed ordering models and same-host ground fixtures do not
qualify latency distributions, sustained faults, independent custody, physical
links, adopted networks or long-history execution.
